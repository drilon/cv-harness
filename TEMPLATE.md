**Purpose:** This file is the single source of truth for AI agents producing a tailored, ATS-compliant CV and cover letter for [YOUR NAME] against a specific job posting.

**Prime directive:** Every line you write must be defensible by the candidate in a live interview. Tailor by SELECTION and EMPHASIS, never by invention. If the job posting demands something not in this file, flag the gap to the user; do not fabricate.

**Second directive:** Honest is the floor, not the goal. The goal is an interview. A reader who skims for ten seconds must be able to see that the candidate meets this posting's top requirements. Relevance to THIS posting decides what leads, what gets space, and what gets cut.

---

## 1. WORKFLOW

1. **Read the posting.** Extract: exact job title, role type and seniority, must-have requirements (up to 6) separated from nice-to-haves, exact keywords and phrasing, the problem this hire is meant to solve, region, hard gates (certifications, work authorization, sector years, location), and application instructions (file format, requested documents, questions to answer).
2. **Check hard gates** against Section 8 (Red Lines & Gaps). If a gate fails, tell the user BEFORE writing anything.
3. **Build the fit map** and show it to the user before drafting. One row per requirement:

   `Requirement | Must-have or nice-to-have | Best evidence (Section 6/7 reference) | MATCH, BRIDGE, or GAP`

   - MATCH: direct evidence in this file.
   - BRIDGE: adjacent, truthful evidence. State the bridge in one line ("no X, but did Y, which shares Z").
   - GAP: nothing in this file. Never fill it.

   Verdict: **STRONG** (every must-have is MATCH or BRIDGE, most are MATCH), **STRETCH** (one must-have GAP, or most must-haves are BRIDGE), **LONG SHOT** (two or more must-have GAPs). On LONG SHOT, stop and ask the user whether the application is worth sending.
4. **Select the playbook** from Section 9 and build the CV by subtraction from the inventory in Section 6. Give space by relevance to this posting: the most relevant role gets the most bullets, even if it is not the longest or the proudest.
5. **Win the top third.** The headline, the summary, and the first two bullets of the most relevant role must between them answer the top 3 must-haves with evidence. Reorder bullets inside every role so the most posting-relevant comes first.
6. **Mirror the posting's wording** wherever truthfully supported. Use its exact phrase, not a synonym, when Section 7 lists the two as equivalent. Each must-have keyword appears at least once inside an experience bullet with evidence, not only in the skills list.
7. **Draft the cover letter** per Section 11, and the short outreach note unless the user declines it.
8. **Answer screening questions** (from the posting or the application form) using Section 14 only.
9. **Run the QA checklist** in Section 12 and show the results, including the keyword coverage table.
10. **Deliver** in the format the posting asks for. If it does not say: .docx if the environment supports file creation, otherwise clean Markdown. Filename: `CV_[Your_Name]_{Company}.docx`. Also provide a plain-text copy for pasting into web forms.

## 2. OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS (ATS COMPLIANCE)

- Single column. No tables, text boxes, images, icons, charts, skill bars, or multi-column layouts.
- Standard headings only: Summary, Core Competencies (optional), Experience, Education, Additional Information.
- **Headline** directly under the contact block: one line chosen from the allowed headlines in Section 5, the one closest to the posting's job title.
- Reverse chronological. Consistent date format with months: `Feb 2024 - Present`.
- Standard font (Calibri or Arial), 10.5-11.5pt body. No content in headers/footers. Plain round bullets only.
- CV length: 2 pages maximum. Cover letter: 1 page, 250-350 words.
- Spell out keyword acronyms at first use, with the acronym in brackets, when they are likely search terms: `Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)`.
- Core Competencies, if used: 8-12 items, only skills the posting asks for and Section 7 supports, in the posting's order of priority and wording.
- Contact block on lines 1-3: name, then `[City, Country] | [phone] | [email] | [linkedin]`.
- Spelling variant: [US / UK], switching to match the employer's region if clearly signaled.
- Job titles, employers, and dates must match the candidate's LinkedIn profile exactly. Recruiters cross-check.

## 3. STYLE RULES

- Voice: [e.g., direct, punchy, minimal hedging]. Strong action verbs. Quantify wherever the registry allows.
- Banned: [e.g., em dashes, the word "passionate", first person in the CV].
- Bullets: 1-2 lines each, built as action + scope + result. Lead with the result when it is the strongest part.
- No duty-only bullets ("Responsible for...") when the inventory holds a result for the same work. If it holds none, state scope concretely (volume, frequency, who used it).
- Summary: 3-4 lines. Line 1 names the role identity in the posting's terms. The rest is evidence, with 2-3 registry numbers. No adjectives without a fact behind them.
- No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no references line.

## 11. COVER LETTER & OUTREACH GUIDE

**Cover letter**
- One page, 250-350 words, addressed to the named person if known, else "Dear Hiring Team".
- Structure: (1) Hook: the role plus your single strongest fit fact. Default hook for [role family A]: [ ]. For [B]: [ ]. (2) Proof: map 2-3 of the posting's top requirements to registry-backed facts, mirroring their vocabulary. (3) Why them + direct close.
- The letter must add something the CV does not: the problem this hire solves, and the one piece of evidence that the candidate has solved it before. Never restate the CV in prose.
- "Why them" uses only facts from the posting or supplied by the user. If there are none, ask the user for one or cut the line. Never invent praise.
- Address a gap proactively only if it is central to the posting; one honest sentence, pivot to strength.
- Never state salary, visa status, or relocation terms unless the user instructs.

**Outreach note** (to a recruiter, hiring manager, or contact at the company)
- 60-90 words, plain text, no attachments assumed. One sentence on the role, one registry-backed proof fact that matches the top requirement, one specific ask (a short call, or who the right person is).
- Leave `[NAME]` for the recipient. Never guess a name or a shared connection.
