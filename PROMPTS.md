# Prompts

Copy-paste prompts for working with your harness. Attach or paste the harness file alongside each one.

## 1. Fit check (run this first, every time)

> Here is my CV harness and a job posting. Run ONLY Steps 1-3 of the harness workflow. Report back: (a) hard gates passed or failed, (b) the fit map: every must-have and nice-to-have rated MATCH, BRIDGE, or GAP with the evidence you would use, (c) the verdict: STRONG, STRETCH, or LONG SHOT, (d) which playbook from Section 9 applies, (e) the problem this hire is meant to solve, in one sentence. Do not draft any documents yet.

Cheap, fast, and it kills dead applications before you spend an evening on them. Put your effort where the verdict is STRONG.

## 2. Full draft

> Proceed per the harness. Deliver: (1) a tailored, ATS-compliant CV per Sections 2-3, built by selection from Section 6 using playbook [A/B/C], (2) a one-page cover letter and the outreach note per Section 11, (3) answers to these screening questions per Section 14: [paste questions, or "none"]. Then display the completed QA checklist from Section 12, including the keyword coverage table, and list anything you flagged rather than included.

## 3. Hostile review (run on the output)

> Act as a skeptical interviewer who has my harness and this CV side by side. Find every claim in the CV that is stronger than the harness supports, every figure that deviates from the registry, and every framing-rule violation. Be pedantic.

## 4. Ten-second review (run on the output)

> Act as a recruiter screening 200 applications for this posting. You have ten seconds and have not seen my harness. Read only the headline, the summary, and the first two bullets of the first role. Tell me: which of the posting's top 3 requirements you can see evidence for, which you cannot, and whether you would move this CV forward. Then name the single change that would most improve the top third, using only facts already in the harness.

Run this in a fresh conversation without the harness attached, so the reviewer sees only what a recruiter sees.

## 5. Parse test

> Convert this CV to plain text exactly as a parser would: strip all formatting, keep reading order. Then extract name, email, phone, location, each job (title, employer, start and end dates), education, and skills into a list. Report anything that came out missing, merged, or in the wrong order.

If a field comes out wrong here, it will come out wrong in an applicant tracking system. Fix Section 2 of the harness, then regenerate.

## 6. Outreach note only

> Using the harness and this posting, write the outreach note per Section 11 for [recruiter / hiring manager / someone I know at the company]. Then give me two alternative first sentences.

Send it. An application that someone inside the company is expecting is far more likely to be read.

## 7. Cover letter only

> Using the harness and this posting, write only the cover letter per Section 11, playbook [X]. Then show which registry facts you used and which requirement each paragraph answers.

## 8. Screening answers

> Here are the application form questions for this posting: [paste]. Answer each from Section 14 of the harness. For any question Section 14 does not cover, do not answer; list it for me. Warn me about any answer that is likely to be an automatic disqualifier.

## 9. LinkedIn sync

> Compare my harness with my LinkedIn profile text below. List every mismatch in titles, employers, dates, and figures. Then draft a LinkedIn headline (from the allowed headlines in Section 5) and an About section built from Section 5 and the registry. [paste profile text]

Recruiters check LinkedIn against the CV. A mismatch reads as a red flag even when both versions are true.

## 10. Interview prep (when you get the call)

> I have an interview for this posting. Using the fit map, list the 8 questions I am most likely to be asked, including one for each BRIDGE and GAP. For each, outline an answer from Section 6 as situation, action, result, using registry figures only. Mark any answer where the harness lacks the detail and ask me for it.

## 11. Update the harness

> New fact: [describe it, with numbers]. Update the harness: add it to the correct Section 6 entry with its result, update Section 10 if any figure changed, and add an expiry note to Section 13 if it will go stale. Show me a before/after diff of the changed lines only.

## 12. New playbook

> I am starting to target [role family]. Read Sections 5-8 and draft a new Section 9 playbook for it: which identity leads, the headline, section order, what to promote and demote, keywords to mirror, the usual gap and its bridge, and any Section 8 red line likely to be tested by these postings. Also propose additions to the equivalent and not-equivalent term lists in Section 7.

## 13. Outcomes review (every 10 applications)

> Review Section 15 of the harness. Compare interview rates by playbook, fit verdict, and whether outreach was sent. Tell me what is working, what is not, and what you cannot conclude because the sample is too small. Propose specific edits to Sections 5, 9, or 11. Do not propose changing any fact.

## 14. Refresh audit (quarterly)

> Audit the harness for staleness. Check every Section 13 item, every dated or tense-sensitive claim in Sections 6 and 8, and every Section 14 answer against today's date, and list every question I need to answer to bring the file current. Also list every Section 6 bullet that has no result, so I can add one.

## Tips

- Always ask for the QA checklist output. The flags are the product.
- If the agent invents anything, do not fix the document. Fix the harness (usually Section 8 needs a new line), then regenerate.
- If the ten-second review fails, do not fix the document either. Fix the playbook or the allowed headlines, then regenerate.
- One conversation per application keeps context clean. The harness is the memory; the chat is disposable.
- Log every application in Section 15 the day you send it. Unlogged applications teach the harness nothing.
